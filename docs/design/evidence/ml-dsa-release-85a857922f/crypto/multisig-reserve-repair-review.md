The prior gap is source-addressed: multisig opening obligations are now preserved as metadata from the actual composed wrapper, carried through adapters and batches, and included with the complete inner fee before Shield adds the carrier fee. Reserve payers and the outer fee payer are aggregated by decoded AccountId, so alternate SS58 prefixes cannot split the same account's obligations. A first `as_multi` approval reserves `DepositBase + threshold * DepositFactor`; a later approval reserves no new deposit. A first outer approval or hash-only approval cannot execute its inner call, so the merge helper discards nested approval obligations while retaining its own. A batch appends each concrete child's approval records rather than overwriting them or deduplicating by payer. Completed branches may include a newly opened nested operation, whose deposit is correctly assigned to the nested dispatch account. No expected release of an existing deposit is subtracted from the required balance before execution. The direct `Multisig.approve(..., shielded=True)` path resolves pending state and constructs one public-only wrapper before unlocking the signer. It quotes that wrapper's inner fee, carrier fee and opening deposit plus the retained balance floor, then submits the same wrapper. Missing preparation/deposit/carrier information fails closed. Threshold-one uses its corresponding runtime call and no opening deposit. Plain direct-group submissions preserve their legacy best-effort affordability behavior. State changes by competing signers after a quote can still make a transaction fail; no snapshot or guarantee of execution was inferred.

The final narrow adjustment separates immediate affordability from eventual authorization: when an outer approval defers the inner call, the merge helper clears the inherited `pure_proxy_reserves` affordability map while retaining `protected_pure_registration_deposit_rao` and the other semantic permanent-spend metadata. Thus a protected group with zero free balance can open the operation when its member funds the immediate approval deposit and both fees, but `max_spend_tao=0` still rejects authorizing future pure creation. The new regression checks both outcomes and verifies that the final approval again requires the group's pure-creation reserves. Preview wording now identifies these as authorized future reserves. Nested and batch composition retain reserves for branches that do execute; no future refund is credited in advance and permanent-spend policy is not weakened.
